Exit edge and Entry edge — the simple version

Using your own example: leave Windows at the right, arrive on Kali at the left. Which means Kali sits to the right of the laptop.

1 · the whole thing

The two settings describe one trip: Windows → Kali

Not two trips. Not one per machine. One trip, and a trip has a place you leave from and a place you arrive at.

    You push the cursor back into that same left edge on Kali. Control goes home. This is the only thing Kali needs the setting for: "which edge means go home". Same edge you arrived at.
2 · your question

"Can I make the arrow come back on the laptop's left side?"

No — and there is no setting for it, on purpose.

Here's why. While you were on Kali, the laptop's cursor never moved. It sat parked at the right edge the whole time, frozen. So when control comes back, it's already there — nothing has to be placed anywhere.

Left: the laptop's cursor, frozen at the exit edge, waiting for you. Right: where you actually are.

And you wouldn't want it anyway

If it came back on the left, the cursor would teleport across your whole 1920px screen every single time you returned. You'd lose it constantly. Coming back where you left is what makes it feel like one big desk.

So the round trip needs exactly two settings, not four: where you leave, and where you land. The way home is the same door you came in by.

3 · who decides

Why only the laptop decides

Simple reason: the laptop is the machine holding the mouse. Kali has no mouse of its own in this setup — it only receives. So Kali cannot decide when a crossing starts, because nothing is crossing from Kali.

QuestionWho answers itWhich setting
When does the mouse leave?Laptop onlyExit edge
Where does it land on Kali?Laptop onlyEntry edge
Which edge sends control home?Kali needs to knowEntry edge (same value)
Where does it come back on the laptop?Nobody — it never left that spotno setting

That's why entry edge exists on both machines and must match: the laptop uses it to place the cursor, Kali uses it to know which edge means "go home". If they disagree, the cursor lands on one side and Kali waits for you on the other.

4 · the removal

We're not removing a setting. We're removing a fake knob.

Nothing you can do today stops working. To be exact about what changes:

  • ✓Exit edge still exists and still works — on the laptop, where it does its job. Untouched.
  • ✓On Kali it is only hidden, because the Kali engine never reads it. Turning that dial today writes a value into the config file that nothing ever looks at. It looks like a control; it does nothing.
  • ✓Entry edge stays on both machines — it's real on both, just for different reasons (place the cursor / send control home).

Think of a car with a steering wheel painted on the passenger door. Removing the painting doesn't stop the car steering — it stops you trying to turn it and wondering why nothing happens.

In one line

Exit edge = the door you leave the laptop by. Entry edge = the door you arrive at on Kali, and the same door you push back into to come home. The way back to the laptop needs no setting, because the cursor never left that spot.
